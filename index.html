About

I joined 1.5°C (umgrauemeio) as an intern, became CTO ten months later, and today I'm co-founder — leading engineering at a Brazilian climate tech company built around one idea: you can't manage a wildfire you don't see coming — prevention, response, all of it starts there.

Tech alone doesn't solve the climate crisis. It needs policy, community, and people willing to do the unglamorous work — that's the part I care about most.

If you're working on something in that same direction — wildfire, climate, agentic AI — I'd like to hear about it.

Pantera

Pantera is the hybrid edge/cloud AI platform we built at 1.5°C, covering the full cycle — prevention, detection, response, and impact assessment. It combines a network of 190 cameras across Brazil with satellite feeds and fire risk modeling to detect wildfires with 95% accuracy, taking on one of the country's largest sources of CO₂ emissions.

17M+ hectares monitored
190 cameras in the field
95% detection accuracy
10mo intern → CTO

Some of that ground is the Pantanal and indigenous territories — the kind of land where losing a few hours of detection time actually matters. We also work with clients in pulp/paper, agriculture, and conservation to cut fire impact on the ground.

Built on Python, FastAPI, AWS, Docker, PostGIS, and PyTorch.

Agents

Alongside Pantera, I set up the infrastructure behind 8 AI agents that now run day-to-day operations across Sales, Customer Success, Support, and Marketing at 1.5°C — orchestrated on Hermes, an open-source agent framework I deployed on our own servers. Each agent owns a domain, and the whole thing runs without anyone babysitting it.

Open source

Since 2022 I've contributed to ForeFire, an open-source wildfire simulation engine in C++ built by CNRS at Université de Corse. Mostly the less glamorous stuff that makes research software usable: Dockerizing it, setting up CI/CD, and writing docs people can actually follow.
